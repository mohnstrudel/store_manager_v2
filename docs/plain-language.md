# Clear communication

Clear communication helps readers understand the main point quickly and know what to do next. Use these rules for architecture and plans, LLM conversations, and interface text.

## Core rules

- Write for the current reader and task. Include what the reader needs to understand, decide, or act.
- Put the answer, result, action, or recommendation first.
- Prefer active voice, present tense, and direct verbs. Name who or what acts.
- Use the shortest familiar word that keeps the meaning exact. Keep necessary business and technical terms, and explain an unfamiliar term briefly when it helps the reader.
- Replace noun phrases with verbs: `validate`, not `perform validation`.
- Cut adverbs and intensifiers that add no information. Keep a qualifier only when it changes the claim.
- Prefer short sentences. Keep one main idea in each sentence, paragraph, or bullet, and split a sentence that carries two.
- Give detail progressively. Put essential information before background, exceptions, and technical detail.
- Remove repetition, filler, and links that do not serve a clear purpose.
- Treat these rules as strong defaults, not mechanical bans. Keep passive voice, longer sentences, adverbs, or qualifiers when they make the meaning more accurate or clear.
